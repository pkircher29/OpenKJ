**Trivia tidbit:** The classic CD+G (Compact Disc + Graphics) format—the red-and-yellow text standard that powered karaoke for over two decades—was co-invented by Philips and Sony in 1985. It was originally designed to display album lyrics and liner notes on TV screens while listening to standard audio CDs, but mainstream music buyers largely ignored it. It sat virtually unused until commercial karaoke manufacturers realized they could use the hidden “subcode” graphics channels to display scrolling lyrics, accidentally creating a multimillion-dollar industry standard from a failed consumer-audio feature.
